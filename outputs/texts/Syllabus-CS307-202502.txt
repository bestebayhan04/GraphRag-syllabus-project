This course covers fundamental aspects of operating systems: management and virtualization
of resources such as CPU, memory space and peripheral devices. Topics include concurrent
processes, mutual exclusion, process communication, cooperation, deadlocks, semaphores,
scheduling, protection, memory management, file system abstraction and persistent memory
organization. The course will also highlight important aspects of operating systems such as
UNIX and Windows.
Course Learning Outcomes:
1. To have a high-level understanding of computer systems, how an operating
system works, and the general structure of an operating system.
2. To have an understanding of how OS handles multpile processes, and multi-
threading including synchronization, scheduling, and memory management.
3. To be aware of some common problems due to interprocess communication, how
these problems are modeled and solved.
4. To have an understanding of how how file systems are implemented and how I/O
operations are performed.
5. To have an understanding of basics of threats to computer security
Course Objective
To supply the students with the theory and practice of operating systems and operating system
design principles.

1. Grading Policies
1.1 Programming Assignments (PAs)
There will be 4 programming assignments in total. You will be required to write C/C++ programs.
Your programs should compile and run properly on UNIX-based operating systems.
For each PA, there will be a related quiz, immediately after the deadline. The quiz score will
determine the maximum score you can take from that PA. For each PA, a margin will be
announced after the quiz to be added to the quiz score to determine the upper threshold for
your PA score. For instance, if your related quiz score is 50 and the announced margin is 20,
then the maximum score you can get from this PA is 70.
If you miss a PA quiz and you want your PA to be graded, you have to take an oral exam to
determine your PA score.
1.2 Midterm Examination
2-3 hours exam towards the end of 8th week. The midterm exam will be physical and in class.
Students will answer both theoretical and computational questions using pen and paper. Exam
date will be scheduled by Student Resources (SR) and cannot be modified by course staff.
1.3 Final Examination
2-3 hours exam that will be held during the finals week Exam date will be scheduled by SR and
cannot be modified by course staff. The final exam will cover all the topics in the course.
1.4 Quiz
There will be 9 quizzes throughout the semester. Top 7 quiz scores will be counted towards the
overall score. Quizzes might take place during lectures or recitations. Quizzes will not be
announced beforehand except the PA quizzes. PA quiz scores are counted towards the quiz
grading item, too.
There will not be any makeup quizzes.
1.5 Oral Examination
For each of the assessment method above, some students might be randomly called for an oral
examination to clarify their work. Depending on the oral exam score, the original assessment
score might be modified.
1.6 Overall Score Restriction
Students whose total score is less than 30 or final exam score (or the make-up exam score that
will replace the final exam) is less than 25 will automatically fail the course (get an F).

2. Office Hour Policy
This semester office hours will be conducted online. To attend the office hours of LAs, TAs and
the instructor (course staff), students must send an email the corresponding course staff at
least a few hours ago. Last-minute appointment requests will not be accepted. Each member
of the course staff will reserve 2 hours per week for conducting office hours. Office hours are
divided into 30-minute slots. Students can request at most one slot per week. Slots will be
booked in a first come first served manner. After all the slots become full, course staff do not
guarantee to open new slots.
3. Email Policy
Course staff are expected to reply to student emails within 48 hours. Course staff might not
always provide immediate responses. It is advised that students do not wait until the submission
deadline for doing their assignments. Course staff try to be more responsive during the deadline
period, but last-minute questions might not be answered due to the heavy email traffic.
Students are advised to post their course related questions to the discussion forum in SUCourse
since other students might answer them quickly. Course staff has the freedom of not answering
a student question if the answer already exists in the course material (syllabus, discussion
forums, course book or any other material in SUCourse) or if the student is expected to find the
answer herself/himself.
4. Grace Day Policy
Each student has 5 grace days in total that can be used throughout the semester for extending
missed deadlines. It can only be used for extending programming assignment deadlines. The
student might use all grace days at once for one assignment or partition them among distinct
assignments. Granularity of using grace days is 1-day. For instance, the student cannot ask for a
2 day 10-hour extension and save 2 days 14 hours for later. The student does not have to
provide any excuse or document for using his/her grace days. If the student finishes all his/her
grace days, no further extension will be given to him/her even if he/she has a valid excuse and
documentation.

5. Makeup Policy
There is only one make-up exam that will be conducted after the final exam. The exam date is
decided and announced after the final exam. Only students with a valid excuse (SU health-
center approved health report or any other official document) for the midterm or the final exam
are eligible for the make-up. See "Student Medical Reports Instruction Letter" for detailed
information. The make-up score can replace only one exam. Even though the student has a valid
excuse for not attending both final and midterm exams, s/he can take the make-up to replace
only one of them.
6. Conflict Policy
Time conflict permissions are not given for lectures or recitations.
7. Academic Integrity & Plagiarism
Cheating and plagiarism will not be tolerated, see Sabanci University’s statement on academic
integrity for more information.
Students’ programming assignment submissions will be compared with other submissions from
both the current and previous terms by the course staff using the JPLAG tool. If the similarity
score exceeds a predetermined threshold, penalties will be applied. If the JPLAG test fails for
the first time, student’s score might be reduced to 0 depending on the degree of similarity. For
the second JPLAG failure, the obtained score will be -100 for both submissions and for the third
failure the student’s case will be directly forwarded to the disciplinary committee for further
investigation. It will not be possible to regain scores of failed tests back through extra
assignments, resubmissions, and oral exams.

7.1. AI Policy
GenAI usage for learning purposes is encouraged. However, copying any AI generated code for
PAs and AI generated text for reports are strictly prohibited. Intensive usage of GenAI by many
students might cause high similarity among submissions and result in JPLAG failures.
Report submission similarities will also be checked via Turnitin. If the similarity scores exceed a
threshold, measures explained for JPLAG failures will be applied.
